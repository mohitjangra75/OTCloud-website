What is Occupational Therapy?

In pediatric care, an 'occupation' is everything a child needs and wants to do daily. When a child struggles with these tasks, an OT helps bridge the gap between their current abilities and their full potential.

What Does an OT Actually Do?

Our therapists analyze the complex interaction between the child, their environment, and the task at hand. We evaluate three primary factors:

Sensory Systems

How the brain processes touch, sound, movement, and visual information.

Motor Performance

Fine motor precision, hand strength, and gross motor coordination.

Self-Regulation

The ability to maintain an appropriate state of alertness for the task.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a typical OT session last?
Most clinical sessions are 45–60 minutes long, depending on the child's age, tolerance, and the specific goals defined in the assessment.
Can OT help with my child's handwriting?
Yes. OTs look at the root cause of handwriting difficulties, including fine motor strength, visual-motor integration, and core stability.
Does my child need a diagnosis to start OT?
No. We work with many children who have "functional delays" or sensory differences but may not meet the full criteria for a clinical diagnosis.
