What Is Occupational Therapy?

Occupational therapy helps children become more independent in the activities they do every day.

For children, these everyday activities include playing, learning, writing, drawing, eating, dressing, grooming, following routines and participating in school.

An occupational therapist looks at the skills behind these activities — such as hand skills, coordination, sensory processing, motor planning, attention, visual-motor skills and self-regulation — and works on them through meaningful, age-appropriate activities.

The goal is simple: helping children participate more comfortably, confidently and independently in everyday life.

Sometimes, the signs are small — but they can make everyday activities much harder.
