      Frequently Asked Questions

      Common questions from parents about developmental behaviors.

      My 2-year-old is not talking yet. Is this normal?
      While every child develops at their own pace, by age 2 most children have a vocabulary of at least 50 words and are starting to combine two words. If your child is not yet using words or gestures to communicate, an evaluation by a speech-language pathologist can help determine if support is needed.
      Why does my child walk on their toes?
      Toe walking can be common in toddlers learning to walk. However, if it persists beyond age 2, it may be related to sensory processing, muscle tightness, or motor planning. An Occupational Therapist can assess whether it's a sensory preference or a physical requirement.
      How can I tell the difference between a tantrum and a meltdown?
      A tantrum is usually goal-oriented (wanting a toy) and stops once the child gets what they want or realizes it won't work. A meltdown is a sensory or emotional overload — the child loses control and cannot stop easily even if the "trigger" is removed.
      What should I do if my child avoids eye contact?
      Avoiding eye contact can be a sign of social-communication challenges or sensory overstimulation. It's best to observe this alongside other behaviors, like how they share interest or respond to their name. A professional assessment can provide clarity.
      Will my child "grow out" of these behaviors?
      Some behaviors are transitional, but "wait and see" can often lead to missed opportunities for early intervention. If a behavior is impacting daily life, learning, or social play, it's always safer and more beneficial to seek professional advice.
